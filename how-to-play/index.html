How to Play Dailygolf

Five free golf games. Three guessing games with a new mystery player every day, a daily trivia quiz and a draft simulator you can play any time.

  • YouTube Golfer and Pro Golfer: guess the mystery golfer in five tries.
  • Internet Invitational: guess the player from this year’s Internet Invitational.
  • Internet Invitational Draft: draft a team and play for the title.
  • Daily Golf Trivia: five golf questions a day.

The guessing games

  1. Pick YouTube Golfer, Pro Golfer or Internet Invitational on the start page.
  2. Start typing a name and choose a player from the list.
  3. Each guess shows a row of clues compared with the mystery player.
  4. Use the clues to narrow down who it is. You have five guesses.

What the colours mean

  • Green: an exact match.
  • Yellow: close (for example, within a couple of years of age, or the same continent).
  • Grey: no match.

Arrows show direction. An arrow pointing up (▲) means the mystery player's number is higher than your guess, and an arrow pointing down (▼) means it is lower.

Guess the Pro Golfer clues

  • Country: green if the same country, yellow if the same continent.
  • Tour: where the player competes, for example PGA Tour, LIV Golf or DP World Tour.
  • Age: calculated from the birth date. Yellow means within two years.
  • Majors: the number of major championships won.
  • Wins: official PGA Tour wins, including majors.

Guess the YouTube Golfer clues

  • Country: green when all countries match, yellow if one matches. Some creators represent two countries.
  • Handicap: the creator's golf handicap. A plus handicap (+) is better than scratch. Yellow means within 1.5.
  • Subs: subscriber count. Yellow means within 1.5 times.
  • Age: where known.
  • Channel: green when both creators appear on the same channel, such as Good Good or Big Wedge Golf.

Guess the Internet Invitational Player clues

  • Team: green when the mystery player is on the same team (Dalke, Bryan, Finch or Ruffels).
  • Handicap: the player’s estimated handicap. Yellow means within 1.5, and the arrow shows if the mystery player’s number is higher or lower.
  • Played 2025: green when both players did, or both did not, play the first Internet Invitational.

Daily and practice mode

A new mystery player appears for everyone every day. Once you finish, come back tomorrow to keep your streak going. If you want more, switch to Practice Mode with the button at the top for random players. Practice rounds do not count towards your stats.

Statistics

Tap the chart button to see games played, win rate, current streak and your guess distribution. Statistics are saved in your own browser, so they stay on your device. See the privacy policy for details.

Internet Invitational Draft

The draft is not a daily puzzle. Play it as often as you like.

  1. Pick your teams. Captain one team, or several and pass the phone to friends. AI captains run the rest.
  2. Draft. A snake draft: the pick order turns around every round, until every team has 16 players.
  3. The draw. One team is drawn at random and picks its semifinal opponent.
  4. Matchups. Every match has a scramble round and an alt-shot round. The captains take turns: one sends out a pair, the other answers with a pair, and they switch every pairing until all 16 players are out.
  5. Results. Each pairing is worth a point, so 16 points per match. Tied 8–8 goes to a sudden-death playoff. The two winners meet in the final, played the same way.

Better handicaps win more often, but upsets happen. In a scramble the better player carries the pair, while in alt shot both players hit every other shot, so a weak partner hurts more.

Daily Golf Trivia

Daily Golf Trivia has five multiple-choice questions every day: four about pro golf and one about golf YouTube. After each answer you see the right answer and a short explanation. Score three or more to keep your streak going. When you are done, play a practice round with new questions.

Tips

  • Start with a well-known player to get a feel for the clues.
  • Use majors and wins together: a zero in both usually means a younger or lesser-known player.
  • Think about the tour first. It removes many players at once.
